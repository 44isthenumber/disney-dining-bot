Shut out of Christmas-week Disney dining at 60 days? What can still reopen

November 4, 2026

You were up before 6 AM Eastern, about 60 days before Christmas Eve or Christmas Day, signed in and ready. The one table your family wanted for the holiday was already gone, or only an odd time was left.

That empty screen is not the final answer. Here's why, what can trip you up, and what to do next.

The calendar math

Disney dining reservations open about 60 days out, at 6 AM Eastern. For 2026, as a worked example: Christmas Eve opened on October 25 and Christmas Day on October 26.

Guests at Disney Resort hotels book their whole stay, up to 10 nights, starting 60 days before check-in. So a resort guest checking in December 20, 2026 could book both holiday meals on October 21, days before an off-site guest's windows opened. We cover how that works in our off-site 60-day guide.

Why tables can come back

Disney's own rule: most restaurants charge a $10-per-person fee only if you cancel within 2 hours of your reservation or don't show. Cancel earlier and, at most restaurants, there's no fee. (Reservations can't be modified within 2 hours of the time.) That gives people room to change plans late.

Some guests also hold extra reservations and cancel the ones they don't use later. Disney may release more inventory later too, and holiday dining packages have been released on a rolling basis.

So tables can reopen in the weeks before, the day before and the same day. Nobody publishes how often or how fast that happens, and there's no promise one opens for your party.

Disney notes its policies are subject to change without notice. Check the current terms before you book.

The traps

Packages, prepaid meals and special events play by their own rules. Their deadlines and penalties can differ from a regular reservation.

Some can't be changed at all once confirmed. Disney lists Cinderella's Royal Table, Hoop-Dee-Doo and the Rose & Crown and Spice Road Table Fireworks Dining Packages among the experiences that allow no modifications after you confirm. Others need a day or more notice to cancel free, including those two fireworks packages and Hoop-Dee-Doo; Disney also cites Victoria & Albert's at 5 days, Takumi-Tei at 2 and Monsieur Paul at 3. Don't hold one of these "just in case."

A dining reservation is not a Candlelight seat. A regular restaurant reservation doesn't include Candlelight Processional show seating. That comes with a Candlelight Processional Dining Package, which is a separate booking.

Read your confirmation email. Each restaurant's cancellation terms are in it.

What you can do yourself

  1. Rank 2 or 3 restaurants and times you'd accept. One perfect table is a hard target; a short list is not.
  2. Widen your time filters. An early dinner or late lunch is easier to find than 6 PM.
  3. Search across days. You can search availability across up to 10 days within your booking window. Christmas Eve, or another day of your trip, can be your holiday meal.
  4. Book a second choice and keep looking. Then cancel what you don't need inside its free window, and never on a no-modify booking you can't afford to lose.
  5. Keep backups as backups. Mobile Dine Walk-Up and the Holiday Kitchens at EPCOT's Festival of the Holidays (Nov 27 to Dec 30, 2026) are extra chances, not the plan.
  6. Remember park admission. A meal inside a park still needs valid park admission, plus a park reservation if your ticket type requires one.
