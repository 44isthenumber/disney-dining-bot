Staying off-site? Why your Disney dining table was gone at 6 AM on day 60

October 28, 2026

You set the alarm. You were signed in at 5:55 AM Eastern, exactly 60 days before the day you wanted Cinderella’s Royal Table, and you refreshed at 6:00. The times you wanted were already gone, or only a very late dinner was left.

If you’re staying off-site, that is probably not a glitch, and it probably wasn’t your timing. It’s how Disney’s booking window is set up.

The rule, in Disney’s words

Disney’s dining FAQ splits guests into two groups:

  • Guests with a valid Disney Resort hotel reservation can book starting 60 days before arrival for their entire stay, up to a 10-night stay.
  • All other guests can book starting 60 days in advance.

planDisney panelists, on Disney’s own planning site, spell out what that means for you: if you’re off-property, you book each day individually as its own 60-day window arrives, at 6 AM Eastern. Disney’s FAQ adds that new booking windows open at approximately 6:00 AM daily for select restaurants.

Why that can beat you to a table

Here’s the math, using dates only as an example, in a year when February has 28 days. A resort guest checks in March 1 for a 10-night stay. At 6 AM on December 31, they can book dining for March 1 through March 10, all at once.

You’re off-site and want March 10. Your window opens on January 9, which is nine days later. If a hard table like Cinderella’s Royal Table was taken in that gap, it was gone before your alarm went off.

This head start is built into how the windows work. We can’t see Disney’s inventory, so we can’t tell you who booked your table. This is the usual explanation, not a certainty.

A case that looks the same

  • Split stay. Per planDisney, if you change hotels mid-trip, your next hotel’s window opens off its check-in date, not the trip’s first day. Dates that fall under a later hotel are booked later.

What you can still do yourself

1. Keep your daily 6 AM check for the next date. Off-site windows open one day at a time, so each morning at 6 AM a new date on your trip opens up. The other days of your trip are still ahead of you.

2. Think before you grab a “placeholder” at Cinderella’s Royal Table. It’s prepaid in full when you book (planDisney). Disney’s page for the restaurant also says a confirmed reservation can’t be modified, and you need to cancel at least 2 hours before your time to avoid the per-person fee. So book a time you’d actually keep. Read the terms on your confirmation before you pay.

3. At restaurants that allow changes, a workable slot can be a fallback. A planDisney answer says dining reservations can generally be modified or cancelled in My Disney Experience, though each restaurant has its own policy, and some guests hold a time they can live with and swap if a better one appears. That’s a guest tip, not a Disney promise, and it doesn’t apply to Cinderella’s Royal Table. Check the terms for your restaurant when you book.

4. Keep checking as your trip gets closer. Disney’s FAQ says that when a guest cancels a restaurant reservation, that time is made available to another guest, and that each restaurant’s cancellation terms are given when you book. A planDisney answer (Dec 2022) describes cancelling 2 or more hours ahead as fee-free, with each location setting its own policy. So a table that’s gone today can sometimes reappear. A planDisney panelist gave the same advice for a restaurant with few openings: keep checking as your visit approaches. That’s no promise a table will open for your party.
